    • Water vs snow. Forecast snow amounts on this page are inches of snow. The longer-range and season sections use inches of water, because that is what the SNOTEL station measures and what long-range models forecast. 1″ of water ≈ 10–15″ of snow, depending on how cold it is.
    • Chance = share of 154 forecasts (51 ECMWF, 51 ECMWF AI, 31 GFS ensemble, 21 Canada GEM, via Open-Meteo) that show at least that much snow. Typical is the middle forecast; high end (“up to”) is the 1-in-10 big outcome; the likely range runs from the 1-in-10 low to the 1-in-10 high.
    • Confidence on the first card combines how far out the storm is and how much the four models disagree on amount and timing.
    • Snow is computed from each forecast’s water, using temperature to set how fluffy it is (5–13 inches of snow per inch of water; above freezing it counts as rain).
    • Summit water is scaled up to correct the models’ measured dry bias at Thaynes Canyon last season.
    • “Town” is a point near the resort base (7,000 ft), not a specific house. Snow varies a lot street to street.
    • Forecasts 8–10 days out catch big storms only about half to two-thirds of the time. Treat the back half as a heads-up, not a promise.
    • Snowpack is measured by the Thaynes Canyon SNOTEL (9,260 ft, on Park City Mountain). It weighs the water in the snow, not new snowfall. The 4-week outlook uses the 31-member GFS ensemble (35-day runs) at the same station.
    • Refreshes twice daily, about 5:45am and 2:45pm MT.
    • Sources: Open-Meteo ensemble forecasts; NRCS Thaynes Canyon SNOTEL #814; NOAA Climate Prediction Center outlooks.
